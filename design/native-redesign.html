Round 1 · for sparring

Subscriptions, reorganised around when you pay.

A redesign proposal for the native app. The orange-and-stone palette stays. The layout, type, surfaces and the way money is shown all change. There are three directions to react to, the one I'd pick, and a list of open questions at the end.

What makes it feel dated

The navigation chrome is already modern: Liquid Glass toolbar, native menus and a SwiftUI form sheet. The content underneath is what looks basic.

Everything is the same box

The total, the "charged soon" cards and every row all use a 1px border with radius.lg on white. With no difference in weight, you can't tell what matters most.

Dark mode is flat

card and background are the same colour (hsl(20 14% 4%)), so in dark mode the cards are just outlines. There's no layering at all.

Numbers are noisy

"149,00 kr." on every row: forced decimals and a trailing currency abbreviation. The price ends up competing with the name.

Subscriptions have no face

The only category colour is a 3px left border. With no icon, logo or monogram, the list reads as a wall of text.

The headline number means nothing

"Total costs" adds weekly, monthly and yearly prices together (2.342 kr in the sample below). Dates show as 05/10/26 when what you want to know is "in 2 days".

Motion everywhere, no focus

Every card fades in, the list fades up and the chips slide in from the right. Since it all moves, nothing stands out.

Three directions

All three use the same data, palette and native chrome. They differ in what the home screen is about.

A · Timeline

The home screen answers "what's due next?"

The headline is one honest number: what you pay per month, with weekly and yearly prices normalised. Below it, a strip of days shows charges as dots in category colours. The list is grouped by time ("Next 7 days", "Later this month", "Later") rather than shown as one flat list.

Wins

  • Built around the job a subscription tracker actually has: avoiding surprise charges
  • Category colour carries meaning in the dots and tiles, not as decoration
  • Replaces the separate "Charged soon" carousel

Costs

  • Needs next-charge dates calculated from billed_at + interval (see product fixes)
  • Sort and "group by category" move to a secondary view
B · Wallet

The home screen answers "where does my money go?"

A summary card splits monthly spend by category in a segmented bar. Upcoming charges sit in a row of chunky cards, and the full list follows below. It's the most colourful and tactile of the three, closest to Apple Wallet or a banking app.

Wins

  • Gets the most out of user-chosen category colours
  • Keeps today's structure (total, upcoming, all), so it's the easiest migration

Costs

  • Busier. Clashing category colours will show
  • Horizontal cards hide everything past the third
C · Pure native

The app looks like part of iOS.

Inset grouped lists like the Settings app, a search field, solid Settings-style icon tiles, and orange used only as the tint. Close to zero custom components. It would likely be @expo/ui List/Section end to end, like the form sheet already is.

Wins

  • Least code to maintain, and it ages with the OS
  • Dynamic Type, accessibility and dark mode come for free

Costs

  • Very little identity. It could be any app
  • Hard to express time or proportion inside a grouped list

My pick: A, borrowing from B and C

The timeline home is the strongest idea because it's specific to subscriptions. The other two each contribute one thing.

From A

Home structure: an honest monthly number, the day strip, and the list grouped by time.

From B

The category bar, moved into a dedicated Insights view where proportion is the point.

From C

Native chrome and forms everywhere outside the home list: glass toolbar, SwiftUI form, system menus.

New: subscription detail

Tapping a row currently drops you straight into the edit form. A detail sheet shows the yearly cost, upcoming charges and how long you've had it. Editing becomes one tap further away.

Add / edit

Still a native SwiftUI Form. A live preview row at the top shows what you're creating, including the per-month equivalent. Interval becomes a segmented control and category becomes colour chips instead of menus.

Empty state

Ghost rows show what the list will look like once it's filled. Quick-add chips for common Danish services prefill the form.

Sign in

Left-aligned, with the app mark at the top. Fields are filled instead of outlined, and buttons are full-width with 16pt continuous corners. "Sign in with Apple" is an open question.

Insights (B's bar, moved)

Spend by category plus a yearly projection. This view also takes over "group by category" and sorting from the ⋯ menu.

Dark, after scrolling

Raised surfaces (#1C1917 on #0C0A09) replace outlines. Orange text switches to a lighter tint (#FB923C) so it stays readable.

Foundations

The palette stays as it is. Everything new below is either a surface layer or a role-specific variant of an existing colour.

Colour

Orange labels mark tokens that are new. The others are existing values in theme.ts. Category colours stay user data. Tiles render them at 14% (light) and 24% (dark) opacity behind the full-strength letter.

Motion and haptics

  • One entrance moment: the monthly amount counts up on first load. Nothing else animates in.
  • Feedback on touch: rows scale to 0.98 with a light haptic. The day strip gives a selection tick as it scrolls.
  • Show what changed: layout transitions when a subscription is added or removed, and when the list regroups. Drop the per-card FadeIn/FadeInRight.
  • Respect Reduce Motion through Reanimated's ReducedMotionConfig.

Product fixes the design depends on

These change behaviour, not only visuals, so I'm calling them out separately.

Normalise to per month

Show week × 52/12 + month + year / 12 as the headline instead of a raw sum. In the sample data that's 2,250 kr/mo rather than a meaningless 2.342 kr.

Roll next-charge dates forward

"Upcoming" is currently !isPast(billed_at), so once a date passes the subscription drops out of "Charged soon" for good. The next charge should be calculated from billed_at + interval. The timeline needs this.

Friendlier money and dates

"149 kr" instead of "149,00 kr.", with decimals only when they're non-zero. "In 2 days" or "14 Oct" instead of dd/MM/yy.

Split settings out of ⋯

Sign out, delete account, theme and sorting are all in one menu today. Move account and appearance settings into their own sheet, behind an avatar button in the toolbar.
